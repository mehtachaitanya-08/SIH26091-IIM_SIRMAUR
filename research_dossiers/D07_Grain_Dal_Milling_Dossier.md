## 1. CLASSIFICATION & MARKET

### 1.1 Official Classification & Codes
- **NIC 2008 Codes:** 
  - `10611`: Grain milling: production of flour, groats, meal or pellets of wheat, rye, oats, corn or other cereal grains [S1].
  - `10613`: Pulse milling / dal processing (dehulling, splitting, polishing of gram, pigeon pea, lentils) [S1].
- **NIC 2025 Codes:** 
  - `106102`: Wheat milling, stone atta chakki operations, and custom cereal grinding [S2].
  - `106103`: Dal milling, pulse dehusking, splitting, and besan manufacture [S2].
- **Statutory Sector:** Manufacturing / Agro-Processing (Micro-Enterprise).

### 1.2 Enterprise Population & National Sizing
- **ASUSE Benchmark:** According to MoSPI Annual Survey of Unincorporated Sector Enterprises (ASUSE 2023-24), rural manufacturing micro-units account for 114.38 Lakh units [S3]. Custom grain mills, atta chakkis, and mini pulse mills represent approximately 7.20 Lakh operational rural establishments [S3].

### 1.3 Catchment Demand & Local Feasibility
- **Feasibility Radius:** 3–6 km radial catchment covering 3 to 6 agricultural villages.
- **Household & Crop Base Required:** A viable custom milling unit requires a minimum localized population of 500 to 800 farming households (approx. 3,000 to 5,000 individuals) cultivating wheat, maize, or pulses [S4].
- **Demand Drivers:** 
  - Rural dietary preference for freshly milled, non-packaged whole wheat flour (*chakki fresh atta*) retaining natural bran and germ.
  - Job-work processing of harvested grain for self-consumption (farmers store raw grain and mill 20–50 kg monthly).
  - Growing commercial demand for custom pulse dehusking (pigeon pea / arhar, chickpea / chana, black gram / urad) to avoid high mandi transport costs.
- **Rural vs. Semi-Urban Dynamics:**
  - *Rural Village Unit:* Predominantly job-work based (toll milling charging ₹3.50 to ₹5.00/kg) with minimal working capital tied up in grain inventory.
  - *Semi-Urban / Mandi Hub:* Hybrid operations combining toll milling with merchant milling (procuring raw grain, cleaning, packaging 5 kg / 10 kg branded atta/dal, and marketing to local grocery stores).

### 1.4 Unserved and Underserved Niches
- **Multi-Millet Grinding (Shree Anna):** High-protein gluten-free flour blends (Ragi, Jowar, Bajra) require specialized hammer mill screens to avoid gummy flour texture; local stone chakkis overheat and glaze.
- **Pigeon Pea (Arhar) & Gram (Chana) Dehusking Job-Work:** Village farmers are forced to sell raw pulses at depressed prices because large dal mills only accept bulk commercial lots; a mini dal mill provides high-margin local custom processing.
- **Clean Bran (Choker) & Broken Pulse (Khanda) Animal Feed:** Packaging high-fibre wheat bran and chana chuni directly for local dairy farmers provides a lucrative secondary revenue stream.
- **How an SC Entrepreneur Spots Niches in the Block:**
  1. Check the local Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) crop acreage data for pulses (chana, arhar, moong) and millets in the block.
  2. Visit 5 existing village atta chakkis and test whether they offer pulse dehusking; over 85% only have flour stones and cannot process whole pulses.
  3. Interview 20 women self-help group (SHG) members on their willingness to buy cleaned, sorted dal locally versus traveling to the tehsil town.

### 3.2 Working Capital Cycle (In Days)
- **Raw Material Inventory Days:** 15 Days (Buffer stock of raw wheat and whole pulses).
- **Processing & Conditioning Days:** 2 Days (Water soaking/drying of pulses, stone grinding).
- **Finished Flour & Dal Stock Days:** 5 Days (Buffer stock awaiting retail sale).
- **Customer Credit (*Udhaar*) / Job-Work Lag:** 8 Days (Custom toll milling is mostly instant cash; short credit to local kirana buyers).
- **Total Working Capital Cycle Length:** `15 + 2 + 5 + 8 = 30 Days` [S8].
- **Operating Working Capital Formula:**
  $$\text{Working Capital Requirement} = \left(\frac{\text{Cycle Days}}{30}\right) \times \text{Monthly Operational Cost} + \text{Rotating Raw Grain Inventory}$$
  $$\text{Working Capital} = \left(\frac{30}{30}\right) \times ₹43,450 + ₹2,56,550 = ₹43,450 + ₹2,56,550 = ₹3,00,000 \text{ [S8]}$$

## 4. UNIT ECONOMICS & PRICING

### 4.1 Throughput & Processing Economics
- **Daily Operating Hours:** 8 hours/day, 25 working days per month (200 operating hours/month).
- **Monthly Grain Processing Capacity:** 18,000 kg (18 MT) split between:
  - Custom Toll Milling (Job Work): 12,000 kg (66.7%)
  - Own Merchant Milling (Branded Sale): 6,000 kg (33.3%)
- **Toll Milling Service Fees:**
  - Wheat grinding: ₹4.00 per kg [S8].
  - Pulse dehusking / splitting: ₹6.50 per kg [S8].
- **Merchant Milling Price Architecture (Official Benchmark):**
  - Raw Wheat Procurement: Mandi MSP ₹2,425 per quintal (₹24.25/kg) [S10].
  - Chakki Fresh Whole Wheat Atta Retail Price: ₹34.00 to ₹38.00 per kg [S8].
  - By-product Yield: Wheat Bran (*Choker*) 10% (sold to local dairy farmers at ₹20.00/kg) [S8].
  - Raw Gram (Chana) Procurement: Mandi MSP ₹5,650 per quintal (₹56.50/kg) [S10].
  - Polished Chana Dal Retail Price: ₹88.00 to ₹95.00 per kg [S8].
  - Pulse By-product: Chana Chuni / Broken Dal (*Khanda*) 25% (sold as cattle feed at ₹28.00/kg) [S8].

### 4.2 Monthly Revenue & Profitability Model (Normalized Operating Month)
- **Revenue Stream 1: Custom Toll Milling (Job Work):**
  - Wheat toll milling: 9,000 kg @ ₹4.00/kg = ₹36,000
  - Pulse toll milling: 3,000 kg @ ₹6.50/kg = ₹19,500
  - Subtotal Job Work Cash Revenue: **₹55,500** [S8].
- **Revenue Stream 2: Merchant Atta & Dal Sales (Gross Margin):**
  - Branded Atta: 4,000 kg @ ₹36.00/kg = ₹1,44,000 (Raw cost: 4,000 kg @ ₹24.25 = ₹97,000; bran credit: 400 kg @ ₹20 = +₹8,000; Gross Margin = ₹55,000)
  - Branded Chana Dal: 2,000 kg @ ₹90.00/kg = ₹1,80,000 (Raw cost: 2,000 kg @ ₹56.50 = ₹1,13,000; chuni credit: 500 kg @ ₹28 = +₹14,000; Gross Margin = ₹81,000)
  - Total Merchant Gross Margin: **₹1,36,000** [S8].
- **Blended Monthly Gross Income:** `₹55,500 (Job work) + ₹1,36,000 (Merchant margin) = ₹1,91,500` [S8].
- **Direct Variable Power & Processing Cost (excluding fixed overheads):** ~₹28,500.
- **Net Operating Surplus:** 
  $$\text{Net Monthly Surplus} = \text{Gross Income} - \text{Total Operating Costs (₹43,450)} - \text{Direct Milling Inputs (₹28,500)} = ₹1,19,550 \text{ [S8]}$$
  *(In conservative job-work only mode, net monthly surplus is ₹22,000 to ₹35,000).*

### 4.3 Break-Even Capacity Utilization
- **Monthly Fixed Overheads:** ₹33,500 (Rent, wages, electricity base, loan interest).
- **Average Service Margin per kg (Job Work Base):** ₹4.50 per kg.
- **Break-Even Processing Volume:** 
  $$\text{Break-Even Milling Volume} = \frac{₹33,500}{₹4.50} = 7,444 \text{ kg per month (approx. 7.4 MT) [S8]}$$
- **Break-Even Capacity Utilization:** Approximately **41.3%** of single-shift milling capacity.

## 5. SEASONALITY MULTIPLIER ARRAY

Milling activity is heavily driven by seasonal crop harvesting and monsoon atmospheric humidity.

| Month | Financial Month | Seasonality Multiplier | Grain Harvest & Milling Dynamics |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---|
| **April** | M1 | **1.45** | **Peak Post-Rabi Harvest:** Fresh wheat arrivals; farmers mill massive quantities for annual household storage |
| **May** | M2 | **1.40** | Sustained post-harvest milling; high demand for chana dal dehusking and besan grinding |
| **June** | M3 | 1.10 | Pre-monsoon stocking; farmers complete grain processing before humidity sets in |
| **July** | M4 | 0.75 | Monsoon rains arrive; atmospheric humidity causes flour clumping; low customer turnout |
| **August** | M5 | **0.70** | **Annual Lean Month:** Peak dampness; high risk of fungal spoilage in whole flour; minimal milling |
| **September** | M6 | 0.80 | Late monsoon humidity clears; gradual recovery of village chakki footfalls |
| **October** | M7 | 1.15 | **Festival Season (Navratri/Diwali):** Surge in besan and refined pulse flour for festive snacks |
| **November** | M8 | 1.20 | **Wedding & Post-Kharif Season:** High milling volume for rural wedding feasts and winter consumption |
| **December** | M9 | 0.95 | Winter season; stable wheat flour and maize/bajra milling demand |
| **January** | M10 | 0.85 | Cold season; moderate household milling requirements |
| **February** | M11 | 0.80 | Pre-harvest lull; household grain reserves nearly exhausted; low milling off-take |
| **March** | M12 | 0.85 | Early harvest arrivals of barley and early wheat; cleaning of storage bins |

- **Seasonality Array:** `[1.45, 1.40, 1.10, 0.75, 0.70, 0.80, 1.15, 1.20, 0.95, 0.85, 0.80, 0.85]`
- **Mathematical Average Multiplier:** 
  $$\bar{M} = \frac{1.45 + 1.40 + 1.10 + 0.75 + 0.70 + 0.80 + 1.15 + 1.20 + 0.95 + 0.85 + 0.80 + 0.85}{12} = \frac{12.00}{12} = 1.00 \text{ [S8]}$$
- **Lean-Month Cash Flow Stress Factor:** 
  $$\text{Stress Factor} = \frac{\text{Lowest Multiplier (August = 0.70)}}{\text{Average Multiplier (1.00)}} = 0.70 \text{ (30% drop below annual base)}$$

## 7. SCHEME FIT & SUBSIDY DECISION RULES

### 7.1 Primary Scheme: NSFDC (National Scheduled Castes Finance & Development Corporation)
- **Target Category:** Scheduled Caste (SC) entrepreneurs possessing valid community certificate [S9].
- **Family Income Ceiling:** Annual family income must not exceed **₹5.00 Lakh** (effective 07 January 2026) [S9].
- **Applicable Tiers for D07:**
  1. *Micro Finance Scheme (MFS):* Project cost up to ₹1.40 Lakh. Promoter equity: 10% (₹14,000); NSFDC covers 90% up to maximum cap of ₹1,25,000 at **6.5% interest rate p.a.** Amortization tenure: 3 years including 3-month moratorium [S9]. Ideal for village custom atta chakki.
  2. *Term Loan Scheme:* Project cost ₹1.40 Lakh to ₹50.00 Lakh. Promoter equity: 10% (₹85,000 on ₹8.5L unit); NSFDC covers 90% (₹7,65,000 on ₹8.5L unit) at **8.0% interest rate p.a.** Amortization tenure: 7 years including 6-month moratorium [S9]. Ideal for integrated commercial dal & flour mill.
- **Delivery Mechanism:** Direct disbursement through State Channelizing Agencies (SCAs).

### 7.2 Secondary Scheme Compatibility & Subsidy Decision Rules
- **PMFME (Pradhan Mantri Formalisation of Micro Food Processing Enterprises):** 
  - *Fit Status:* **HIGHLY COMPATIBLE / HIGH-SUBSIDY MATCH** [S20].
  - *Subsidy:* **35% credit-linked capital subsidy** on eligible project cost, capped at **₹10.00 Lakh per unit** [S20].
  - *Own Contribution:* Minimum 10% promoter equity.
  - *Applicability:* Pulse milling and regional cereal flour milling are primary One District One Product (ODOP) candidates across dozens of districts in UP, MP, and Bihar [S20].
- **PMEGP (Prime Minister's Employment Generation Programme):** 
  - *Fit Status:* **ELIGIBLE / ALTERNATIVE MATCH** [S21].
  - *Category:* Agro-based Food Processing Manufacturing.
  - *Subsidy:* **35% margin money subsidy** for rural SC entrepreneurs; promoter contribution is only **5% of project cost** [S21]. Maximum project cost allowed: ₹50 Lakhs.
- **Combinability Rule:** Capital subsidy double-dipping is prohibited under Government of India financial guidelines. An entrepreneur cannot combine PMFME 35% grant with PMEGP 35% grant. However, an entrepreneur can utilize the NSFDC Term Loan at 8% as the underlying institutional loan to apply for PMFME or PMEGP capital subsidy where the State Channelizing Agency is recognized by the lending bank and nodal agency [S9, S20].

## 8. RISKS, SWOT & COMPETITOR DENSITY

### 8.1 Operational Risk Register & Algorithmic Mitigation Rules

| Risk Factor | Severity | Early Warning Indicator | App Algorithmic Decision Rule (If-Then) |
|:---|:---:|:---|:---|
| **Motor Burnout Due to Low Voltage / Power Cuts** | High | Rural 3-phase line voltage dropping below 360V (or phase imbalance >15%) | `IF line_voltage < 370V THEN trigger auto-cut on DOL starter AND display alert: halt heavy dal dehusking to prevent copper coil burnout until grid voltage normalizes.` |
| **Monsoon Flour Moisture & Fungal Glazing** | High | Relative humidity >80% in July–August; ground flour clumping in hopper | `IF ambient_humidity > 75% THEN reduce daily grinding batch size by 50% AND activate infrared hopper heating lamps to prevent mold growth in storage bins.` |
| **Emery Stone Glazing & Grinding Inefficiency** | Medium | Grinding time per 50 kg bag increases by >25%; flour discharge temperature >55°C | `IF milling_duration_per_bag > 1.25 * base_time THEN prompt operator: schedule emery stone redressing chiseling within 48 hours to restore sharp grinding grooves.` |
| **Dust Explosion & Respiratory Health Hazard** | Medium | Suspended flour dust concentration visible in shed lighting; dry season | `IF dust_cyclone_filter_pressure_drop > 20% THEN halt grinding operations AND mandate shaking of exhaust fabric filter bags to maintain laminar air suction.` |

### 8.2 SWOT Analysis

#### Micro Finance Tier (₹1.40 Lakh Setup)
- **Strengths:** Low debt burden (₹1,25,000 @ 6.5%); essential non-discretionary village utility service; immediate daily cash flow.
- **Weaknesses:** Solely dependent on custom toll grinding; no merchant margin leverage; vulnerable to local power outages.
- **Opportunities:** Introducing localized besan (chana flour) and multi-millet grinding during festival months.
- **Threats:** Expansion of subsidized PDS flour distribution by state governments reducing private milling volumes.

#### Term Loan Tier (₹8.50 Lakh Setup)
- **Strengths:** Dual income stream (custom toll milling + commercial packaged dal/atta sales); high processing efficiency with automated cleaning and destoning.
- **Weaknesses:** Higher electricity overheads; working capital required to buy and hold raw pulse stocks.
- **Opportunities:** Supplying packaged 5 kg flour/dal bags to rural retail stores, Anganwadis, and Mid-Day Meal school contracts.
- **Threats:** Severe price spikes in raw pulses (chana/arhar) eroding packaged product margins.

### 8.3 Competitor Density & Market Estimation Methodology
- **Data Source for Density Mapping:** District Industrial Center (DIC) MSME Udyam Database & Panchayat Commercial Registers [S3].
- **Estimation Metric:** 
  $$\text{Target Milling Density} = \frac{\text{Catchment Farming Households}}{150 \text{ to } 200 \text{ households per viable atta chakki}}$$
- **Rule of Thumb:** If more than 3 atta chakkis exist in a single village (population <2,000), the toll milling market is saturated. The entrepreneur must install a **Mini Dal Mill attachment** or grain destoner to offer specialized services that traditional chakkis cannot provide.
